Water Intake Calculator — How Much Water Do You Actually Need Each Day?

Why Hydration Matters for Fitness

Water makes up about 60% of your body weight and is involved in nearly every bodily function — digestion, nutrient transport, temperature regulation, joint lubrication, and muscle contraction. Even mild dehydration (as little as 1–2% of body weight) can reduce strength, endurance, and mental focus.

For anyone exercising regularly, staying properly hydrated isn't optional — it directly affects performance and recovery.

How Your Weight and Activity Affect Water Needs

There's no single answer for everyone. A larger body needs more water to function. A more active body loses more through sweat. Hot climates increase losses further. This calculator accounts for all three factors to give you a personalized daily target.

  • Base need: 0.033 L per kg of bodyweight
  • Light exercise: +0.35 L
  • Moderate exercise: +0.59 L
  • Intense training: +0.95 L
  • Hot climate: +0.5 L additional

Signs You're Not Drinking Enough

  • Dark yellow urine (pale yellow = well hydrated)
  • Headaches, especially in the afternoon
  • Fatigue or low energy mid-day
  • Difficulty concentrating or brain fog
  • Feeling hungry shortly after eating (thirst is often mistaken for hunger)

Tips for Drinking More Water

  • Start every morning with a large glass of water before coffee
  • Keep a reusable bottle visible on your desk — out of sight means forgotten
  • Drink a glass before each meal as a habit anchor
  • Add lemon, cucumber, or mint if plain water feels boring
  • Don't wait until you're thirsty — thirst is a late sign of dehydration

FAQ

Does coffee count toward my water intake?

Yes — caffeine has a mild diuretic effect, but coffee and tea still contribute net hydration. Plain water is best, but caffeinated drinks do count.

Can you drink too much water?

Yes — hyponatremia (water intoxication) occurs when you drink extreme amounts in a short time, diluting blood sodium. For most people this isn't a concern, but don't force yourself to drink far beyond your target.

Should I drink more on rest days?

Slightly less than training days, but still prioritize hydration. Your body continues repair and recovery on rest days and still needs adequate water.

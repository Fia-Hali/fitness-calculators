What Is Intermittent Fasting?

Intermittent fasting (IF) is not a diet — it's a pattern of eating. Instead of changing what you eat, IF changes when you eat. By restricting your eating to specific windows of the day, you naturally reduce calorie intake and give your body extended periods to burn stored fat.

Unlike calorie counting, many people find IF easier to sustain because the rules are simple: eat during your window, fast outside of it.

The 4 Main Intermittent Fasting Protocols

  • 16:8 — fast 16 hours, eat within an 8-hour window. Most popular and beginner-friendly.
  • 18:6 — fast 18 hours, eat within 6 hours. More effective for fat loss, but stricter.
  • 20:4 — sometimes called the "Warrior Diet." Very narrow eating window, harder to sustain.
  • 5:2 — eat normally 5 days, restrict to ~500 calories on 2 non-consecutive days.

For most beginners, 16:8 is the best starting point — it usually just means skipping breakfast.

Who Benefits Most from Intermittent Fasting?

IF tends to work best for people who:

  • Aren't hungry in the morning and can skip breakfast easily
  • Prefer fewer, larger meals over frequent small ones
  • Want structure without calorie counting
  • Are already in a maintenance or slight surplus and want to cut without major diet changes

IF is not ideal for people with a history of disordered eating, those with high activity demands, or pregnant/breastfeeding individuals.

Common Intermittent Fasting Mistakes

  • Overeating in the eating window — IF doesn't give you unlimited calories; portion size still matters
  • Breaking the fast with junk food — the quality of what you eat during your window still determines results
  • Doing intense training while fasted — fine for low-intensity work, but heavy training often suffers
  • Starting with 20:4 immediately — start with 16:8 and work up gradually

What Breaks a Fast?

During your fasting window, these are safe: water, plain black coffee, plain tea, sparkling water. Avoid: anything with calories — milk, sweeteners, juice, gum, or supplements with carbs.

FAQ

Will I lose muscle on intermittent fasting?

Not if your protein intake is adequate. Research shows IF preserves lean mass similarly to traditional calorie restriction — especially when combined with resistance training.

Can I work out during the fasting window?

Yes, many people train fasted with great results. For heavy strength training, some prefer to eat before — try both and see what works for your performance.

How long before I see results from IF?

Most people notice reduced appetite and weight changes within 1–2 weeks. Significant fat loss typically shows at 4–8 weeks, depending on your overall calorie intake.

TDEE Calculator — Know Your Maintenance Calories Before You Diet

What Is TDEE?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total number of calories your body burns each day, including everything from breathing to exercise. It's the single most important number to know before starting any diet or fitness plan.

Eat below your TDEE and you lose weight. Eat above it and you gain. Eat at your TDEE and you maintain. It's that simple.

How TDEE Differs for Sedentary vs. Active People

Two people of identical weight and height can have very different TDEEs based on their activity level. A sedentary office worker might burn 1,800 calories daily, while someone who trains 6 days a week could burn 2,800+. Choosing the wrong activity multiplier is one of the biggest reasons people plateau.

  • Sedentary (×1.2) — desk job, little to no exercise
  • Lightly active (×1.375) — walking, light gym 1–3x/week
  • Moderately active (×1.55) — training 3–5x/week
  • Very active (×1.725) — hard training 6–7x/week
  • Extra active (×1.9) — physical job + daily training

Common Mistakes That Skew Your TDEE

  • Overestimating activity — most people should pick one level lower than they think
  • Never recalculating — TDEE drops as you lose weight; update every 10 lbs
  • Ignoring non-exercise movement — standing, walking, and fidgeting all count

Is This Calculator Accurate?

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which research shows is more accurate than the older Harris-Benedict formula for most adults. It's an estimate — real-world factors like sleep, stress, and hormones also affect calorie burn. Use it as a starting number and adjust based on 2–3 weeks of real results.

Tips for Using Your TDEE Effectively

  • Recalculate every time your weight changes by 10 lbs
  • If you're not losing on a 500-cal deficit, your activity level may be set too high
  • Use your TDEE alongside a macro split for complete nutrition planning
  • Track food for at least 2 weeks before adjusting — give the data time to show a trend

FAQ

What's the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the calories you burn at complete rest. TDEE multiplies BMR by your activity level to estimate total daily burn.

Should I eat at my TDEE to build muscle?

A slight surplus of 200–300 calories above TDEE is the typical recommendation for lean muscle gain — enough to fuel growth without excessive fat gain.

Why does my TDEE change over time?

As you lose or gain weight, your body burns fewer or more calories. Muscle mass also raises your TDEE, which is why strength training supports long-term fat loss.

Protein Intake Calculator for Muscle Gain and Fat Loss

Why Protein Is the Most Important Macro

Protein does more than build muscle — it keeps you full, supports recovery, preserves lean mass during a cut, and has the highest thermic effect of any macronutrient (meaning your body burns more calories digesting it). Whether your goal is fat loss or muscle gain, getting enough protein is non-negotiable.

How Much Protein Do You Actually Need?

Protein needs vary based on your goal. Research supports these general ranges per pound of bodyweight:

  • Fat loss — 1.0–1.2 g/lb: higher protein protects muscle while in a deficit
  • Maintenance — 0.7–1.0 g/lb: enough to maintain current muscle mass
  • Muscle gain — 1.2–1.6 g/lb: extra protein supports new muscle synthesis

These ranges are based on total bodyweight. If you carry a lot of body fat, using lean body mass may give more accurate targets.

Common Protein Mistakes

  • Not eating enough while cutting — low protein during a deficit causes muscle loss, not just fat loss
  • Front-loading protein at dinner — your body can only use so much per meal; spread it out
  • Relying only on protein shakes — whole food sources provide additional nutrients
  • Forgetting plant proteins — beans, lentils, tofu, and tempeh all count toward your daily total

Is This Calculator Accurate?

The ranges are based on established sports nutrition research and guidelines from organizations like the ISSN. Individual needs vary depending on age, training intensity, and body composition — but these ranges work well as a starting framework for most people.

FAQ

Can eating too much protein be harmful?

For healthy individuals, high protein intakes (up to 2g/lb) are well-tolerated. People with existing kidney conditions should consult a doctor before significantly increasing protein.

Does protein timing matter?

Total daily intake matters most. That said, spreading protein across 3–4 meals optimizes muscle protein synthesis throughout the day.

Should vegetarians eat more protein?

Many plant proteins are incomplete or lower in leucine. Vegetarians and vegans often benefit from aiming toward the higher end of the recommended range.

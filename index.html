How These Calculators Work

All formulas are based on peer-reviewed research and widely validated equations:

Mifflin-St Jeor Equation Used for BMR and TDEE calculations. More accurate than the older Harris-Benedict formula for most adults.
US Navy Circumference Method Uses waist, neck, and hip measurements to estimate body fat — no expensive equipment needed.
MET Values (Exercise Science) Metabolic Equivalent of Task values are used to estimate calorie burn for different activities.
3,500 Cal/lb Rule The established estimate used to calculate how long weight loss will take based on your daily deficit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these calculators accurate?

They use validated formulas and provide reliable estimates for most people. No formula is 100% precise for every individual — use results as a starting point and adjust based on real-world progress over 2–3 weeks.

Do I need to create an account?

No — all calculations happen instantly in your browser. No data is stored or sent anywhere.

Which calculator should I start with?

Start with TDEE. It's the foundation number that feeds into calorie deficit, macro split, and weight loss timeline calculators.

How often should I recalculate?

Recalculate your TDEE and related numbers every time your weight changes by 10+ lbs, or if your activity level changes significantly.

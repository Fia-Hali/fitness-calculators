BMI Calculator — What Your Number Really Means (And Its Limits)

What Is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple number calculated from your height and weight. It's used as a general screening tool to categorize people into weight ranges — underweight, normal, overweight, and obese. It requires no equipment and gives an instant snapshot of whether your weight is in a healthy range for your height.

BMI Categories Explained

  • Below 18.5 — Underweight (may indicate nutritional deficiency)
  • 18.5–24.9 — Normal weight (associated with lowest health risks)
  • 25–29.9 — Overweight (increased risk for some conditions)
  • 30 and above — Obese (higher risk for metabolic disease)

Why BMI Is a Flawed Metric

BMI has a major blind spot: it doesn't distinguish between fat and muscle. A bodybuilder with low body fat can register as "obese" by BMI, while someone with a normal BMI can carry dangerous levels of visceral fat. BMI also doesn't account for age, sex, ethnicity, or where fat is stored on the body.

Despite its flaws, BMI remains useful as a quick, population-level screening tool — just not as a standalone health verdict.

When to Look Beyond BMI

  • If you lift weights regularly — muscle skews BMI upward
  • If you're over 60 — slightly higher BMI is often protective in older adults
  • If you're very short or very tall — BMI scales non-linearly with height

Use BMI alongside body fat percentage and waist circumference for a more complete picture.

Tips for a Healthy BMI Range

  • Focus on body composition (fat vs. muscle), not just the scale
  • Strength training builds muscle and improves BMI more sustainably than cardio alone
  • A BMI in the normal range doesn't guarantee good health — diet quality matters too
  • Waist-to-height ratio is often a better predictor of metabolic risk than BMI

FAQ

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

No — athletes with high muscle mass often have elevated BMI scores despite being very lean. Body fat percentage is a better metric for athletes.

Can BMI be used for children?

There are age- and sex-specific BMI charts for children. The standard adult BMI formula should not be used for kids under 18.

What's a good BMI to aim for?

For most adults, a BMI between 20 and 23 is associated with the lowest health risks — but individual context always matters.

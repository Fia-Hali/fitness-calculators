Calorie Deficit Calculator for Fat Loss (Simple & Accurate)

What Is a Calorie Deficit?

A calorie deficit happens when you consume fewer calories than your body burns in a day. This forces your body to use stored fat for energy, which leads to weight loss over time. Understanding your calorie needs is the foundation of any successful fat loss plan.

Our calorie deficit calculator estimates how many calories you should eat daily based on your age, weight, height, and activity level — no guesswork required.

How Many Calories Should You Eat to Lose Weight?

Your daily calorie target is based on your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) — the total calories your body burns each day. For most people:

  • −250 cal/day → about 0.5 lb lost per week (mild, sustainable)
  • −500 cal/day → about 1 lb lost per week (most recommended)
  • −1000 cal/day → about 2 lbs per week (aggressive, harder to maintain)

This calculator estimates your TDEE and subtracts your chosen deficit automatically.

Common Mistakes When Cutting Calories

  • Eating too little — slows your metabolism and causes muscle loss
  • Overestimating activity — leads to a smaller deficit than expected
  • Not tracking consistently — small daily mistakes add up
  • Expecting linear results — weight fluctuates daily; judge weekly trends

Is This Calculator Accurate?

It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation — one of the most validated BMR formulas available. No formula is perfect for every body, but this gives a reliable starting point for most people. Re-calculate every time you lose 10+ lbs, as your TDEE changes with your weight.

Tips for Better Fat Loss Results

  • Prioritize protein — it keeps you full and protects muscle
  • Don't cut below 1,200 cal/day (women) or 1,500 cal/day (men)
  • Combine your deficit with strength training for better body composition
  • Weigh yourself at the same time each day and use weekly averages

FAQ

How fast can I safely lose weight?

Most people lose 0.5–1 lb per week on a moderate deficit. Faster loss is possible but increases muscle loss risk.

Can I lose weight without exercising?

Yes — diet alone creates the deficit needed. Exercise helps maintain muscle and makes results more visible.

Why did I stop losing weight?

Plateaus happen as your body adapts. Try reducing calories by 100–150 more, or adding a short walk daily.

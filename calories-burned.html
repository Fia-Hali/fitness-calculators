Calories Burned Calculator — Why Exercise Calorie Estimates Vary So Much

How Exercise Calorie Estimates Work

This calculator uses MET values — Metabolic Equivalent of Task — to estimate calorie burn. MET is a measure of exercise intensity relative to rest. An activity with a MET of 8.0 burns 8 times more calories than sitting still. Multiply MET by your weight and duration and you get an estimate of calories burned.

Formula: Calories = MET × weight (kg) × duration (hours)

Why Calorie Estimates Vary Between People

Two people doing the same workout can burn very different numbers of calories. Here's why:

  • Body weight — heavier people burn more calories for the same effort
  • Fitness level — fitter people perform the same work more efficiently, burning slightly fewer calories
  • Age and sex — these affect resting metabolic rate and muscle mass
  • Workout intensity — running at 8 mph burns significantly more than a jog at 5 mph, even over the same time

This is why your treadmill says 400 calories but your friend's says 550 for the same session.

Common Mistakes When Using Calorie Burn Data

  • Eating back all exercise calories — this often leads to no deficit at all, stalling fat loss
  • Trusting gym machine displays — most cardio machines overestimate burn by 15–30%
  • Only doing cardio — strength training burns fewer calories per session but raises your resting metabolic rate long-term
  • Not accounting for rest periods — weight lifting MET applies to active lifting time, not total gym time

Best Exercises for Calorie Burn

  • Jump rope — highest calorie burn per minute of any common exercise
  • Running (6+ mph) — excellent sustained calorie burn
  • HIIT — high burn during and after (EPOC effect)
  • Swimming — full body, joint-friendly, high calorie burn
  • Cycling — sustainable for long durations, good total burn

FAQ

Should I subtract calories burned from my calorie target?

If your TDEE already accounts for your activity level, no. If you calculated a sedentary TDEE and exercise on top of that, you can add back 50–75% of the estimated burn to avoid too large a deficit.

Does strength training burn as many calories as cardio?

Per session, usually less. But muscle tissue burns more calories at rest — so consistent strength training raises your TDEE over time, making it powerful for long-term fat loss.

Why does HIIT show a lower MET than running?

HIIT MET values represent average intensity across work and rest intervals. The after-burn effect (EPOC) can add 6–15% more calories burned post-workout, which this calculator doesn't include.

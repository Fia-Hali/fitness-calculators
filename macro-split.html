What Are Macros?

Macros — short for macronutrients — are the three main nutrients that make up your calories: protein, carbohydrates, and fat. Every food you eat is a combination of these three. Knowing how to split them based on your goal can dramatically change your results.

  • Protein — 4 calories per gram. Builds and preserves muscle.
  • Carbohydrates — 4 calories per gram. Primary energy source.
  • Fat — 9 calories per gram. Hormones, brain function, satiety.

Which Macro Split Is Right for Your Diet?

There's no single best macro split — it depends on your goal and dietary preference:

  • Balanced (30/40/30) — great all-around starting point for most people
  • High Protein (40/30/30) — ideal for muscle gain or preserving muscle while cutting
  • Keto (25/5/70) — very low carb, forces the body to burn fat for fuel

Not sure which to pick? Most beginners do well starting with the balanced split and adjusting from there.

Common Macro Mistakes

  • Ignoring fat intake — dietary fat is essential; don't cut it to zero
  • Not hitting protein — the most commonly under-eaten macro, especially on keto
  • Forgetting liquid calories — drinks like juice, milk, and alcohol have macros too
  • Switching splits too often — give any approach at least 4–6 weeks before judging

Is This Calculator Accurate?

The gram values are calculated directly from your calorie target using the standard calorie-per-gram values (protein: 4 cal/g, carbs: 4 cal/g, fat: 9 cal/g). The splits themselves are based on widely recommended ranges. Individual needs vary — adjust based on hunger, energy, and progress.

Tips for Hitting Your Macros

  • Track with a food app like MyFitnessPal for the first few weeks
  • Prep protein sources in bulk — chicken, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese
  • Fat is calorie-dense — a little goes a long way, so measure oils and nuts
  • Prioritize protein first at each meal, then fill in carbs and fat around it

FAQ

Do I have to hit macros exactly every day?

Not exactly — aim to be within 5–10g of your targets. Consistency over days matters more than perfection on any single day.

Can I do keto and still build muscle?

Yes, but it's harder. Keto works better for fat loss. For muscle gain, higher carbs typically support better performance and recovery.

What if I go over my fat or carb target?

Adjust the rest of the day — eat lighter protein-focused meals to compensate. One bad meal doesn't ruin progress.
